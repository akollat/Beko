How can we ensure AI is good for everyone in manufacturing?

AI has the potential to make manufacturing more efficient. But as its use grows, we want to make sure everyone can benefit, especially women.

Women currently make up just 27% of the UK manufacturing workforce. New research commissioned by Beko shows they are disproportionately represented in some of the occupations most exposed to generative AI.

Our new report, The Default Setting: Women, AI and the Future of Work in UK Manufacturing, found that an estimated 41% of women's manufacturing employment is in occupations exposed to generative AI, compared with 23% of men's. Women also account for 63% of those working in the most exposed roles.

The research was led by Zoi Roupakia, Founder and Director of Noetic AI and a Policy Affiliate at Cambridge Industrial Innovation Policy. The study combines a survey of 890 UK manufacturing professionals with analysis of ONS employment data and international evidence on occupational AI exposure.

Rather than suggesting AI is going to replace women's jobs, the report highlights why responsible AI governance is vital. If AI is going to create new opportunities, employers need to think carefully about who is most affected, how they can be supported, and where human guidance remains important.

At Beko, we're already using the findings to strengthen our approach to responsible AI and people practices, including better governance, training and human decision making.
